What this model family is

A Capable Open-Weight AI Built for Production, Privacy, and Practical Scale

Google builds Gemma for one purpose: serious capability at a size that runs without a data center. The Gemma family — spanning Gemma 2, Gemma 3, and the latest Gemma 4 — delivers instruction following, reasoning, and multilingual understanding that competes with models twice its size.

For organizations that have watched Google's consumer AI products harvest user data, Gemma is the answer: the same engineering discipline applied to a model you actually own. On NoCloudGPT, Gemma runs privately on a small cloud server or local Linux machine with no telemetry, no usage caps, and no data leaving your control.

Who developed it

Google DeepMind

[Google DeepMind developer artwork / logo — 320×80 recommended]

Gemma is developed by Google DeepMind, the AI research division formed by the merger of Google Brain and DeepMind in 2023. The Gemma family was first released in February 2024 as a set of open-weight models built on the same research and infrastructure used to develop Google's Gemini model series, made available specifically to support the broader open-weight AI community.

Google releases Gemma weights under the Gemma Terms of Use, a custom license designed to permit broad use and redistribution while retaining certain conditions. VERIFY: Confirm current Gemma license terms — the specific conditions and any restrictions on commercial use or redistribution may vary between Gemma 2, Gemma 3, and Gemma 4 generations and should be reviewed before production deployment.

Which size should you choose?

Matching Gemma Model Size to Your Project

Gemma is available in several parameter sizes across multiple generations. Smaller models run on very modest hardware and respond quickly but may struggle with complex instructions or lengthy documents. Larger models produce higher-quality output but require more RAM and compute. VERIFY: Confirm current available Gemma 3 and Gemma 4 model sizes and Ollama tags before publishing.

Actual performance also depends on quantization level, context length in use, concurrent users, and the specific hardware you are running on. The guidance below is directional. NoCloudGPT will help you size the right configuration for your workload before the pilot begins.

Small models (Gemma 2B / 3B / 4B)

Starter / Edge pilot

These run on very modest hardware, including a 2–4 GB Lightsail instance or a modern laptop. Fast responses and low memory requirements make them practical for proof-of-concept pilots, simple Q&A and classification tasks, and edge or disconnected deployments where hardware is severely constrained. Output quality and instruction-following are more limited than larger models.

Medium models (Gemma 7B / 9B / 12B)

Practical default

The 9B range is where most NoCloudGPT Gemma deployments begin. A 4–8 GB Lightsail instance handles this tier comfortably and responds at interactive speeds for daily business use. Instruction following, summarization, multilingual writing, and internal Q&A all perform noticeably better than the small models. This is the recommended starting tier for teams moving from a commercial AI tool to a private deployment.

Large models (Gemma 27B)

GPU / On-premise planning

Noticeably stronger reasoning, analytical depth, and writing quality compared to the 9B tier. Runs on an 8 GB Lightsail instance or entry GPU server at Q4 quantization. The right choice for organizations that need daily-use quality closer to frontier models for demanding document analysis, structured reasoning, or professional writing tasks — without committing to a full GPU cluster.

Gemma 4 multimodal

GPU instance recommended

Adds vision understanding for teams whose documents contain images, scanned forms, charts, or diagrams. A GPU instance is recommended for practical response speeds at the multimodal tier. This is the right path when your document processing pipeline includes non-text content and you want a single private deployment to cover both. VERIFY: Confirm Gemma 4 hardware requirements and Ollama availability at time of deployment.

Honest guidance

When to Choose a Different Model Instead

Gemma is a strong general-purpose starting point, but it is not always the best choice for every specific use case. Here is where to consider alternatives.

Your primary use case is software development or code review

CodeGemma, DeepSeek Coder, and Qwen Coder are purpose-built for software engineering tasks and typically outperform a general Gemma model on code generation, debugging, and code review at equivalent sizes. See DeepSeek Coder →

You need advanced mathematical or scientific reasoning

DeepSeek's reasoning-focused models are specifically trained for mathematical reasoning, chain-of-thought analysis, and structured problem-solving workloads. For quantitative analysis, financial modeling, or scientific work, DeepSeek R1 typically outperforms a general Gemma model at comparable sizes. See DeepSeek →

You need the widest size range for a pilot that grows without switching families

Qwen runs from 0.5B to 72B within a single model family. If you need a very small pilot model and a large production model in the same family — with the same tooling and system prompts — Qwen's size range is wider than Gemma's current generation. See Qwen →

Your primary need is high reasoning quality on the smallest possible hardware

Microsoft's Phi models are specifically designed to deliver high reasoning quality at small parameter counts. If hardware constraints are severe and reasoning accuracy matters more than broad knowledge, Phi may deliver better results than a small Gemma at the same memory footprint. See Phi →

You need private semantic search or document retrieval

Gemma is a generative model, not an embedding model. For building private search over internal documents, you need a dedicated embedding model alongside or instead of a generative model. See Nomic Embed →
