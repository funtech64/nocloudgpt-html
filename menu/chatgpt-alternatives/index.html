What are ChatGPT alternative models?

After OpenAI released ChatGPT in late 2022, the AI research community immediately started reverse-engineering what made it so capable — and building open versions anyone could run. Three distinct lineages emerged.

Distillation models were trained directly on outputs from GPT-3.5 and GPT-4. Stanford's Alpaca, UC Berkeley's Vicuna, and Microsoft's Orca family showed that a small open model trained on synthetic GPT-4 data could match 90%+ of ChatGPT's performance at a fraction of the cost.

RLHF-trained models replicated OpenAI's Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback methodology using open datasets. HuggingFace's Zephyr, Berkeley's Starling, and Intel's Neural Chat proved you don't need OpenAI's closed infrastructure to produce a genuinely helpful, safe, instruction-following model.

OpenAI's own open releases — GPT-2 and Whisper — are the only models where OpenAI itself made the weights fully public. GPT-2 predates ChatGPT by three years and represents OpenAI before the company decided open weights were a liability.

All of these run on Ollama. All work with Open WebUI out of the box. None of them require an API key, a subscription, or a server you don't control.

Questions
What people ask before switching

Are these models actually as good as ChatGPT?
For most everyday tasks — writing, summarization, Q&A, coding help, email drafting — yes. Modern open models like Zephyr 7B and OpenChat 3.5 outperform ChatGPT 3.5 on standard benchmarks. They are behind GPT-4 on complex multi-step reasoning. The gap closes every three months. For 80% of what you use ChatGPT for today, a local model is good enough right now.
What hardware do I need?
A 7B model runs on any laptop with 8GB of RAM. No GPU required — though a GPU makes it significantly faster. 8GB VRAM handles 7B models with room to spare. 16GB VRAM handles 13B models comfortably. See our quickstart guide for exact requirements by model.
Is this legal? These models were trained on ChatGPT data.
The legality of distillation-trained models is genuinely unsettled. OpenAI updated their terms of service to prohibit using their outputs to train competing models — after many of these were already built. The models themselves are freely distributed and used commercially by thousands of companies. Running them is legal in every jurisdiction we're aware of. Training new models on ChatGPT outputs today would violate OpenAI's ToS.
How do I connect these to Open WebUI?
Install Ollama, pull any model, install Open WebUI via Docker. Open WebUI auto-detects all Ollama models. You get a ChatGPT-identical interface running entirely on your machine. Full walkthrough at deploy/quickstart.
Which model should I start with?
If you want the closest ChatGPT 3.5 experience: Zephyr 7B or OpenChat 3.5. If you need reliable professional outputs with low hallucination: Nous Hermes 2. If you want maximum capability regardless of size: WizardLM 70B or Vicuna 33B. If you have a standard laptop: Dolphin Mistral 7B.
Why does Dolphin say "uncensored"? Is that safe?
"Uncensored" means the model follows your instructions without the corporate refusal patterns that make ChatGPT say things like "I can't help with that" for perfectly legitimate requests. A doctor researching drug interactions, a novelist writing a thriller, a security researcher testing vulnerabilities — all hit ChatGPT's walls constantly. Dolphin is designed for professional users who need complete instruction compliance. It is not a tool for generating harmful content — that depends on the user, not the model.
