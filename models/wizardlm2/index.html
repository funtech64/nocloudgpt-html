What Is WizardLM2?

WizardLM2 is an instruction-tuned language model family from Microsoft Research, built using a training technique called Evol-Instruct. Instead of training on a static dataset of human-written instructions, Evol-Instruct uses a language model to iteratively rewrite simple instructions into progressively more complex, constrained, and nuanced versions — generating training data that covers a far wider range of instruction difficulty than human annotation alone can produce.

The result is a model unusually capable of handling demanding, multi-step instructions: tasks with many constraints, detailed specifications, complex conditional logic, or multiple interdependent requirements. Where standard instruction-tuned models tend to simplify or partially ignore complex inputs, WizardLM2 was specifically trained to follow them faithfully.

WizardLM2 is available in 7B and 8x22B (Mixtral-based MoE) variants — deployable via Ollama for private, on-premise use. The announced 70B variant was never publicly released.

What Evol-Instruct means for you: Most instruction-tuned models are good at simple commands and struggle as instructions get more specific and complex. WizardLM2 was trained specifically on progressively harder instructions — so tasks that would cause a standard Mistral Instruct to drop constraints or go off-spec are more reliably handled by WizardLM2. This matters most for automated pipelines, structured output generation, and multi-step document workflows.

WizardLM2 8x22B

141B MoE

Built on Mixtral 8x22B. The full-power variant for complex reasoning, long-form generation, and demanding instruction-following tasks. Requires substantial GPU infrastructure but delivers output quality competitive with frontier models on structured tasks.

Where WizardLM2 Outperforms Standard Instruction Models

Complex Multi-Step Instructions

Give WizardLM2 a prompt with ten specific requirements and it follows all ten. Standard instruction-tuned models increasingly drop constraints as prompts grow more complex. WizardLM2's Evol-Instruct training specifically targets this failure mode — making it reliably useful for workflows where precision matters.

Structured Output Generation

When you need output in a specific format — structured JSON, formatted reports, tables, code with particular specifications — WizardLM2 adheres to format requirements more consistently than models without this specialized training. For document automation and data extraction pipelines, reliable formatting reduces the need for post-processing.

Multi-Turn Task Execution

WizardLM2 maintains context and constraint adherence across multi-turn conversations better than many alternatives — relevant for interactive workflows where users refine and extend instructions across multiple exchanges rather than issuing everything in a single prompt.

Reasoning Through Constrained Problems

Tasks that require working through multiple constraints simultaneously — planning, analysis with specific scope limitations, content generation with numerous style and content rules — benefit from WizardLM2's training distribution. The model has seen this kind of problem more than models trained on simpler data.

Who This Model Family Is For

👤 Personal use

WizardLM2 is a strong choice if you want a local AI that reliably follows complex, multi-step instructions without drifting off-topic. Its Evol-Instruct training makes it particularly good at handling layered requests — such as “rewrite this in our tone, flag risks, then draft a reply.” The 7B version runs comfortably on a modern consumer GPU or Apple Silicon Mac, making it practical for journaling, drafting, study help, and long back-and-forth conversations entirely offline. Newer families like Llama 3.3 and Qwen 2.5 have since pulled ahead on general knowledge and coding, so treat WizardLM2 as a capable, disciplined workhorse rather than a cutting-edge flagship.

🏢 Business / team use

Teams that need consistent instruction following across long, multi-turn conversations will find WizardLM2 unusually reliable. It excels at structured tasks such as summarizing documents to a spec, following formatting rules, and maintaining tone across extended interactions. The 8x22B mixture-of-experts variant delivers near-frontier reasoning quality on your own hardware — useful for internal knowledge assistants and document processing where data cannot leave the building. Its training data predates mid-2024, so it performs best when reasoning over documents you provide rather than answering questions about current events.

⚙️ Developer & technical use

Developers building agents, tools, or structured-output applications benefit from WizardLM2's strong ability to follow nested instructions, edge-case specifications, and complex formatting requirements. The 7B version serves as a solid base for prototyping on modest hardware, while the 8x22B handles more demanding reasoning and orchestration tasks. For pure code generation or the absolute latest benchmark performance, dedicated coding models or newer generalists like DeepSeek V3 and Qwen 2.5-Coder will outperform it. Position WizardLM2 as your reasoning and orchestration layer rather than your primary code completer.

🔬 Data science & research

WizardLM2 is genuinely interesting from a research perspective as one of the most successful public demonstrations of Evol-Instruct and AI-supervised post-training. It serves as a useful baseline for studying instruction-tuning methods and the economics of mixture-of-experts inference. The 8x22B variant is a practical case study in achieving frontier-adjacent quality at lower active-parameter cost. Because it is built on older base models (Mistral and Llama 2 era), it is better suited to reasoning-method research than knowledge-cutoff-sensitive evaluations.

Licensing Information

WizardLM2 was released by Microsoft Research in April 2024 under the Apache 2.0 License (for both the 7B and 8x22B variants). Shortly after release, Microsoft withdrew the official Hugging Face repositories, citing a missed internal toxicity-testing step. The original Apache 2.0 license grant on the released weights is generally considered irrevocable by the community, and the models remain widely available through community mirrors and Ollama.

In practice, the Apache 2.0 license permits commercial use, modification, and redistribution without royalties. However, because the official repositories were withdrawn and no updated safety card was published, risk-averse organizations may prefer model families with active vendor backing.
