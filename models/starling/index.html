Starling

Starling-LM is a 7B conversational model from UC Berkeley that uses Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF) on top of OpenChat 3.5. It achieved an 8.09 MT-Bench score at release — one of the strongest conversational results in the 7B class — while running efficiently on modest hardware.

What Is Starling?

Developed by researchers at UC Berkeley, Starling-LM introduced Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF) — using GPT-4-generated preference rankings from the Nectar dataset instead of human annotators to align the model for high-quality conversation.

Built on OpenChat 3.5 (Mistral 7B), Starling inherits strong instruction-following and adds preference alignment on top. The result was an 8.09 MT-Bench score from a 7B model — competitive with much larger chat models at the time of its release.

For organizations that need a private, conversational assistant for internal Q&A, documentation, or lightweight customer-facing chat — Starling delivers strong dialogue quality at minimal infrastructure cost.

Who This Model Family Is For

👤Personal use

Starling is a strong choice if you want a genuinely conversational local AI that feels helpful and responsive. Its RLHF/RLAIF training on top of OpenChat gives it excellent instruction-following and a natural tone, making it pleasant for everyday writing, brainstorming, summarization, and general chat. It runs efficiently on modest hardware. However, for heavy math, advanced coding, or complex multi-step reasoning, newer models have clearly moved ahead.

🏢Business / team use

Small teams that need a private, low-overhead assistant for internal drafting, document Q&A, or customer-facing FAQ responses will find Starling practical and easy to work with. It follows workplace-style instructions reliably and produces natural-sounding output with relatively little prompt engineering. It is not the best fit if your workload is dominated by structured data analysis, code generation, or technical problem-solving — newer families like Qwen 2.5 or Llama 3.3 will serve you significantly better in those areas.

⚙️Developer & technical use

Developers building conversational interfaces, internal chatbots, or documentation assistants will appreciate Starling's predictable behavior and strong instruction adherence. It integrates cleanly with Ollama and responds well to structured prompts. For code-generation tools, agentic pipelines, or anything requiring strong reasoning chains, more recent models are clearly superior. Starling remains useful for rapid prototyping or adding a polished conversational layer on modest hardware.

🔬Data science & research

Starling earned recognition for its strong MT-Bench scores and remains a useful historical reference for studying the effects of advanced RLHF/RLAIF training on conversational quality at the 7B scale. Researchers exploring instruction-following behavior or preference alignment may still find it valuable as a baseline. For current research requiring state-of-the-art reasoning or coding performance, newer model families have largely superseded it.
