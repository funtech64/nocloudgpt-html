Why All-MiniLM Is Still the Default RAG Embedding Choice

When developers build their first RAG pipeline, they almost universally reach for All-MiniLM-L6-v2. When enterprises audit their production RAG stacks, they frequently find All-MiniLM already running in the embedding layer — often placed there by an engineer who needed something that worked immediately and never got replaced because it kept working well.

This is not an accident. All-MiniLM is the product of Microsoft's sentence-transformers research, distilled from a much larger model down to 22 million parameters without sacrificing the semantic understanding that makes embeddings useful. The result is a model that generates 384-dimensional embedding vectors in milliseconds on CPU hardware, handles the semantic similarity tasks that RAG pipelines depend on with genuine accuracy, and requires so little compute that it can run alongside a generation model on the same modest instance without contention.

For organizations building private document search, internal knowledge bases, customer support retrieval systems, or any RAG-based application where data cannot leave the organization's network, All-MiniLM delivers the embedding layer reliably, efficiently, and at essentially zero marginal infrastructure cost.

All-MiniLM Variants on Ollama

All-MiniLM-L6-v2

Recommended — Speed

6-layer transformer, 22M parameters, 384 dimensions. The standard choice for the vast majority of RAG deployments. Generates embeddings at several hundred documents per second on CPU. Quality is strong for general English semantic search.

ollama pull all-minilm RAM: ~90MB · Dims: 384

All-MiniLM-L12-v2

Higher Quality

12-layer variant, 33M parameters, same 384-dimensional output. Approximately 15–20% better on semantic similarity benchmarks at roughly double the inference time. Useful when retrieval quality is the primary constraint.

ollama pull all-minilm:l12 RAM: ~130MB · Dims: 384

Which variant to choose: Start with L6-v2. It handles the overwhelming majority of enterprise RAG use cases without compromise. Upgrade to L12-v2 only if retrieval quality testing reveals meaningful gaps on your specific document corpus — the speed difference matters at scale when embedding large document libraries.

Who This Model Family is For

👤 Personal use

All-MiniLM is a great fit if you want to build a personal semantic search tool or a local "chat with my notes" system without needing a powerful GPU. It runs comfortably on everyday laptops and mini PCs, and can index thousands of notes or PDFs in minutes. Keep in mind it only produces embeddings — it does not generate answers on its own. You will pair it with a chat model (such as Llama or Mistral) to complete a RAG pipeline.

🏢 Business / team use

For small teams that need a private knowledge base search or internal RAG system, All-MiniLM offers one of the lowest-cost ways to get started. It is lightweight enough to run alongside other workloads on a single modest server, making it practical for validating whether semantic search delivers value before investing in larger models. It performs best on English-language content and smaller to medium-sized corpora.

⚙️ Developer & technical use

Developers building RAG prototypes, search features, or recommendation systems will appreciate that All-MiniLM produces compact 384-dimensional vectors that are cheap to store and very fast to query. The small footprint means you can run the embedder in the same container or process as your application without significant resource overhead. It is a reliable default when you prioritize predictable latency and simplicity over maximum retrieval quality.

🔬 Data science & research

All-MiniLM remains useful as a fast, well-documented baseline for clustering, topic modeling, deduplication, and similarity studies on English text. It is widely cited and easy to reproduce, making it a sensible reference point when benchmarking newer models such as BGE-M3 or Nomic Embed v1.5. On modern MTEB benchmarks it trails larger models, so it is best positioned as a speed-optimized baseline rather than a state-of-the-art result.

Understanding All-MiniLM's Limitations

All-MiniLM is excellent at what it was designed for. It is important to understand where those boundaries are before committing to it as a long-term embedding layer.

256-token input limit. This is the most significant practical constraint. L6-v2 was trained with a 256 subword token maximum, which equates to roughly 180–200 words of English text. Documents longer than this must be chunked before embedding, and the chunk boundary strategy directly affects retrieval quality. For most RAG pipelines this is manageable, but it requires deliberate attention to chunking design.

English-optimized. All-MiniLM performs well on English and adequately on closely related European languages, but it was not designed for multilingual use. Organizations with document corpora in non-European languages should evaluate Paraphrase-Multilingual, BGE-M3, or Qwen3 Embedding instead.

384 dimensions may underfit complex corpora. For large, semantically diverse document libraries — enterprise wikis spanning dozens of domains, research paper corpora, multi-product documentation sets — the 384-dimensional space can become crowded and retrieval precision can suffer. Models like Nomic Embed or BGE-Large at 768 dimensions often show meaningful retrieval improvement on these cases.

When to Upgrade from All-MiniLM

All-MiniLM is the right starting point for almost every RAG project. Consider upgrading to a larger embedding model when: (1) retrieval recall testing shows consistent misses on semantically relevant documents, (2) your corpus spans multiple domains with significant vocabulary differences, (3) you need multilingual support, or (4) your documents average longer than 200 words and chunking is creating context fragmentation problems.

No GPU Required

All-MiniLM does not benefit meaningfully from GPU acceleration for typical RAG workloads. The model is small enough that CPU inference is fast, and GPU overhead for small batch sizes can actually reduce throughput. Run it on CPU and reserve GPU resources for your generation model.

Deployment with Ollama

# Pull All-MiniLM via Ollama
ollama pull all-minilm

# Pull L12 variant for higher quality
ollama pull all-minilm:l12

# Generate embeddings via Ollama API
curl http://localhost:11434/api/embeddings \
  -d '{
    "model": "all-minilm",
    "prompt": "Private AI deployment for enterprise document search"
  }'

# Batch embed multiple documents
curl http://localhost:11434/api/embed \
  -d '{
    "model": "all-minilm",
    "input": [
      "Document chunk one text here",
      "Document chunk two text here",
      "Document chunk three text here"
    ]
  }'

# LangChain integration (Python)
from langchain_community.embeddings import OllamaEmbeddings
embeddings = OllamaEmbeddings(model="all-minilm")
vectorstore = Chroma.from_documents(docs, embeddings)

# LlamaIndex integration (Python)
from llama_index.embeddings.ollama import OllamaEmbedding
embed_model = OllamaEmbedding(model_name="all-minilm")

Use Cases Where All-MiniLM Fits Best

  • Internal knowledge base search: HR policy documents, IT runbooks, product documentation, and company wikis. All-MiniLM handles the vocabulary of a single organization's domain exceptionally well and does so on a 2GB Lightsail instance that costs less than $10/month.
  • Customer support RAG: Embedding product manuals, FAQ libraries, and support ticket histories to power a private support assistant. The speed allows real-time similarity search even on modest hardware.
  • Legal document retrieval: Embedding contract clauses, case law summaries, and regulatory text for semantic search. Keep sensitive legal documents entirely on-premise while enabling AI-assisted research.
  • Research paper corpora (single domain): Academic teams embedding papers within a narrow field. All-MiniLM works well when the vocabulary is relatively consistent. For cross-domain or very large corpora, step up to BGE-Large.
  • Email and communication archives: Embedding historical email threads, meeting notes, and Slack exports for organizational memory search. Short texts play to All-MiniLM's strengths.
  • Product catalog semantic search: E-commerce and inventory systems where users search by meaning rather than exact keywords. All-MiniLM's speed at scale supports real-time product search on CPU hardware.
  • Prototype and MVP RAG pipelines: The fastest path from zero to a working private RAG system. All-MiniLM requires no GPU, no complex setup, and no tuning to produce useful results on day one.
