Reflection 70B: Self-Correcting Reasoning Model

Self-Reflection Reasoning Error Correction 70B

Historical context: Reflection 70B generated significant controversy on release in late 2024 — initial benchmarks were disputed, and independent testing showed mixed results. The underlying technique (reflection tokens for self-correction) is real and interesting, but evaluate it carefully against your specific use case rather than relying on benchmark claims. The model is available and the approach is worth knowing about.

Reflection 70B, released by Matt Shumer and HyperWrite, introduces a specific training technique: the model is fine-tuned to use special reflection tokens (<reflection>) during generation to pause, review its reasoning, identify errors, and correct itself before producing a final answer. The idea is to build self-error-detection into the generation process itself rather than requiring external verification. Built on Llama 3.1 70B, it targets tasks where reasoning accuracy matters more than generation speed.

What Reflection 70B Is Good At

  • Self-correcting multi-step reasoning — the reflection mechanism catches some classes of reasoning errors mid-generation
  • Tasks with verifiable answers — math, logic, and code where the model can check its own work
  • Reducing overconfident wrong answers — the reflection process encourages uncertainty acknowledgment
  • Research and experimentation — interesting as a technique to study; the approach is being extended in newer models

Deployment

Reflection 70B runs on the same hardware as Llama 3.1 70B — two A100 40GB GPUs or equivalent 80GB VRAM total at 4-bit quantization: 

ollama run reflection:70b-q4_K_M
