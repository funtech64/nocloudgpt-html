What this model family is

An Open-Weight Model Built for Genuinely Multilingual AI Work

Aya is a family of open-weight multilingual language models developed by Cohere. The Aya project started as a research initiative involving thousands of researchers worldwide building multilingual instruction data for underrepresented languages — languages that other AI models treat as afterthoughts. The resulting models cover 100+ languages with training data quality that reflects genuine multilingual depth, not just English translated into other languages.

For organizations with operations, customers, or content across multiple languages — particularly in regions that major AI providers underserve — Aya provides better output quality in those languages than general-purpose English-primary models.

Who developed it

Cohere — Aya Initiative

[Cohere / Aya — developer badge artwork]

Aya was developed by Cohere For AI, Cohere's nonprofit research organization, in collaboration with a large open research community. The Aya initiative aimed to address the significant gap in multilingual AI quality for underrepresented languages by building high-quality instruction datasets through community contribution and then training models on that data.

Why teams choose it

Organizations with teams or customers in regions where Swahili, Hindi, Arabic, Portuguese, or other non-Western languages are primary communication channels find Aya significantly more useful than English-first general models at those languages.

Support for underrepresented languages

Research-grade multilingual data provenance

The Aya dataset was built through a transparent community research process with documented multilingual contributors. For organizations that care about training data provenance for their multilingual AI, Aya has clearer documentation than most alternatives.

Who this model family is for

Who This Model Family is For

👤 Personal use

Aya is a strong choice if you regularly work across many languages, especially lower-resource ones that most open models handle poorly. Whether you're communicating with family abroad, learning new languages, translating documents, or simply wanting one private assistant that doesn't default to English-only behavior, Aya offers genuinely broad multilingual capability on your own hardware. For purely English-language tasks, however, newer families like Llama 3.3 or Qwen 2.5 generally feel more polished and capable.

🏢 Business / team use

Teams with international operations, global customer support, localization needs, or multilingual documentation will find Aya particularly valuable. It enables you to build or prototype chatbots and internal tools that serve specific geographic markets without maintaining separate models for every language. If your workflows are primarily English-only, you will usually get better overall performance and consistency from Qwen 2.5 or Llama 3.3. Aya's real advantage is its breadth across many languages rather than peak performance in any single one.

⚙️ Developer & technical use

Developers building multilingual applications — such as translation tools, content moderation systems, or customer support interfaces serving diverse regions — will appreciate Aya's unified coverage across 100+ languages. It reduces the need to chain multiple language-specific models. That said, it is not the strongest choice for English-heavy or code-intensive applications. For those use cases, newer generalist or coding-specialized models will deliver better results.

🔬 Data science & research

Researchers working on low-resource languages, cross-lingual transfer, or multilingual NLP will find Aya a useful baseline because of its extensive training across many languages that are often underrepresented in other open models. It provides a practical reference point for evaluating performance on languages that most models struggle with. For English-centric or coding-focused research, newer model families have generally surpassed it.
