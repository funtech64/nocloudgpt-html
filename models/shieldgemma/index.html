ShieldGemma — AI Safety Classification That Runs on Your Infrastructure, Not Google's

ShieldGemma is Google's open safety classifier — a model specifically trained to evaluate whether AI inputs and outputs contain harmful content across defined safety categories. Deploy it as a local guardrail layer in your private AI pipeline without routing user conversations to an external safety service.

When an organization deploys a private AI system — whether a customer service chatbot, an internal assistant, a content moderation tool, or a public-facing product — they take on responsibility for what that system outputs. A private LLM running on your infrastructure has no external safety layer. The model's own alignment training is the only guardrail, and for most open-weight models, that alignment is imperfect and variable across different prompt patterns.

ShieldGemma provides a second layer of defense. It is a 2B to 9B classifier — trained specifically to evaluate text for harmful content across Google's defined safety taxonomy — that can be inserted into an AI pipeline as an input filter, output filter, or both. Before a user's message reaches your main LLM, ShieldGemma can evaluate it for prompt injection attempts, jailbreak patterns, and harmful content requests. Before the LLM's response reaches the user, ShieldGemma can evaluate it for dangerous instructions, sexually explicit content, harassment, and other defined harm categories. If the classifier flags a message, the pipeline can route it to a refusal response, a human review queue, or simply log it for analysis.

The critical differentiator for private AI deployments is that ShieldGemma runs on your infrastructure. Using an external safety API — even Google's own Perspective API or similar services — means every user conversation, including the sensitive context that often surrounds harmful content requests, passes through a third-party server. For applications handling legally privileged conversations, confidential business discussions, or any personal information, this creates the same privacy exposure that motivated the private AI deployment in the first place. ShieldGemma eliminates this by running the safety classification entirely locally.

What ShieldGemma Classifies

ShieldGemma is trained against a specific set of harm categories derived from Google's safety policy framework. Understanding what it classifies — and what it does not — is important for building accurate expectations about what a ShieldGemma deployment will and will not catch.

The primary classification categories include: dangerous content (instructions for weapons, self-harm, or illegal activities that could enable real-world harm), sexually explicit content (material inappropriate for general audiences), harassment and hate speech (targeted personal attacks and content denigrating protected groups), and prompt injection attacks (attempts to override or manipulate the system's instructions through user input). ShieldGemma can evaluate both the input side — what a user sends — and the output side — what the AI generates in response.

What ShieldGemma does not replace: it is a classifier, not a comprehensive ethical review system. It evaluates against defined categories and will not catch every possible harmful output — particularly subtle manipulation, factually incorrect but not overtly harmful content, or harms specific to domain contexts it was not trained on. It is one layer in a defense-in-depth approach to AI safety, not a complete solution.

Where ShieldGemma Consistently Delivers

Input filtering for customer-facing AI

Before user messages reach your main LLM, ShieldGemma screens for prompt injection attempts, jailbreak patterns, and overtly harmful requests. The classification adds minimal latency (ShieldGemma 2B processes in under a second) while catching the highest-risk input patterns before they consume expensive main model inference.

Output validation before delivery

ShieldGemma evaluates LLM outputs before they are sent to users, flagging responses containing dangerous instructions, explicit content, or harassment. For applications using open-weight models without strong safety alignment — or models where jailbreaks are a known risk — output filtering provides a backstop against the most damaging failures.

Audit logging and compliance evidence

ShieldGemma classification results, logged with conversation IDs and timestamps, create an auditable record of safety decisions in your AI system. For organizations subject to AI Act compliance requirements, internal governance reviews, or litigation discovery, this log demonstrates that a safety layer was operating and what it classified.

Replacing external moderation APIs

Organizations currently using OpenAI's moderation endpoint, Perspective API, or similar cloud safety services can replace those API calls with local ShieldGemma inference. The privacy improvement is significant — no conversation content leaves the network — and the per-classification cost drops to infrastructure cost alone.

Multi-tenant platform guardrails

SaaS platforms offering AI features to customers need a safety layer that operates on customer data without exposing that data to third parties. Private ShieldGemma deployment provides safety classification for every tenant's conversations on the platform's own infrastructure, keeping customer data fully contained.

Research and red-team evaluation

Security researchers and AI safety teams evaluating model vulnerabilities can use ShieldGemma to systematically classify outputs across adversarial test suites without sending potentially sensitive test prompts to external APIs. The local deployment allows unrestricted red-team testing against the classifier itself and against the main model combination.

Who This Model Family Is For

👤 Personal Use

Individuals building personal AI assistants, home automation tools, or small private applications will rarely need a dedicated safety classifier — the overhead is disproportionate for personal deployments with a single trusted user. The exception is developers building and testing AI applications for public use who want to validate their safety layer before launch, or researchers conducting adversarial red-team testing on their own models. For those scenarios, ShieldGemma 2B is compact enough to run alongside any generation model without meaningful hardware overhead.

🏢 Business & Team Use

Any organization offering an AI assistant, chatbot, or AI-generated content feature to end users faces reputational, legal, and operational risk from harmful outputs. ShieldGemma provides a technical safeguard that complements the generation model's intrinsic alignment — and does so entirely on your infrastructure. For organizations in industries where a single harmful AI output triggers regulatory scrutiny or press coverage, this classification layer is not optional. Companies preparing for EU AI Act compliance will find that ShieldGemma deployment with audit logging provides documented technical evidence of implemented safety measures — a specific requirement in the Act's high-risk AI provisions.

⚙️ Developer & Technical Use

Developers deploying open-weight models that have been fine-tuned, merged, or modified from their original safety-aligned form need an external classification layer precisely because those models' internal guardrails are less reliable. ShieldGemma integrates into AI pipelines as an input filter, output filter, or both — the 2B variant adds sub-second classification latency, making it compatible with real-time applications. Developers replacing cloud moderation APIs (OpenAI Moderation endpoint, Perspective API) with ShieldGemma gain both privacy improvement — no conversation content leaves the network — and a per-classification cost reduction to infrastructure cost alone.

🔬 Data Science & Research Use

AI safety researchers and red-team evaluation teams can use ShieldGemma to systematically classify outputs across adversarial test suites without sending potentially sensitive test prompts to external services. Local deployment allows unrestricted testing against the classifier itself. Google's well-documented safety taxonomy — dangerous content, sexual content, harassment, prompt injection — makes ShieldGemma a clean baseline for research comparing safety classifier performance across different model families and fine-tuning approaches.
