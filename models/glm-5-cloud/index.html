What GLM-5 is

GLM-5 is a large mixture-of-experts model from Z.ai (Zhipu AI), with 744B total parameters and 40B active per token. It targets complex reasoning, coding, and agentic workflows — including long-form drafting, dense document summarization, and structured analysis. Ollama lists native support for English and Chinese, configurable thinking/reasoning modes, tool use, and text-only input with a 198K context window on the cloud tag.

On Ollama today, GLM-5 is available as a cloud-only family: glm-5:cloud (high usage tier). That makes Terminal Glass a practical way to use the model without local weights sized for a 744B-class MoE. can host other GLM family builds — see GLM-5 private deployment and GLM family options for hardware-fit alternatives.

The Terminal Glass trade-off

Terminal Glass gives you the same OpenWebUI chat experience and locally stored RAG index you would use on a private deployment — but routes each inference request to Ollama Cloud. Your server runs the Ollama client, OpenWebUI, and your document index; a small cloud VM, NUC, or mini-PC is usually enough. You do not need multi-GPU servers sized for a 744B-class model to start working with GLM-5.

Honest reality: Every prompt and retrieved RAG chunk leaves your infrastructure and is processed on Ollama Cloud. This is not air-gapped and is not equivalent to NoCloudGPT Private Instances, where inference stays on hardware you control. You also pay recurring, usage-based Ollama Cloud fees that grow with context length, reasoning depth, and team chat volume. When sensitive or regulated material must never leave your network, choose a NoCloudGPT Air Gapped deployment instead.

What about the video card?

This is the question most buyers are actually trying to answer.

Some teams want to avoid buying, sizing, cooling, or maintaining GPUs entirely. For them, Terminal Glass Cloud is often the cleaner choice — you get capable models through a familiar OpenWebUI interface without taking on the hardware project.

Other teams prefer to run models on hardware they control — whether for cost at scale, data residency, compliance, or a deliberate air-gapped policy. For those teams, a NoCloudGPT Air Gapped deployment is usually the better path.

There isn’t a single right answer. The right answer depends on your constraints around cost, privacy, compliance, latency, and long-term ownership.

How Terminal Glass Works with This Model

You interact through OpenWebUI on your Terminal Glass host (or via the command line). Chat history, user accounts, and your locally built RAG document index stay on hardware you control. When you submit a prompt, the request — including retrieved passages — is forwarded to Ollama Cloud where GLM-5 runs; the response streams back to your browser.

Cloud access requires an Ollama account (ollama signin) on the Terminal Glass machine. GLM-5 is rated high usage on Ollama — expect higher per-request cost than smaller cloud models, especially with long 198K contexts and reasoning-enabled prompts. 

ollama signin
ollama run glm-5:cloud

What leaves your infrastructure: prompt text and RAG-retrieved passages. GLM-5 is text-only on Ollama. Latency includes network round-trips — acceptable for chat, more noticeable in rapid automation. Cost is usage-based and can accumulate with heavy daily volume or shared team use.

Honest Guidance: When to Choose a Different Path

  • Regulated, confidential, or strict data-residency requirements — Choose NoCloudGPT Private Instances with a local GLM-family model. Terminal Glass routes prompts to Ollama Cloud.
  • High-frequency automated workflows or low-latency needs — Local inference on owned hardware performs better than cloud round-trips.
  • Predictable high or sustained volume — Recurring cloud usage costs may exceed owned inference hardware over many months.
  • Model-version pinning or audit-grade reproducibility — A pinned local deployment on NoCloudGPT Private Instances provides more control than a cloud-only tag.
  • Languages beyond English and Chinese — GLM-5 is optimized for bilingual EN/ZH work. Broader multilingual needs may fit Qwen 3.5 Cloud or another family better.
  • Clinical or diagnostic use — GLM-5 is a general-purpose model, not a validated medical device. Do not use it for patient care decisions without clinical governance your compliance team approves.
