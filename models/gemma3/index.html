What this model is

Google's Recommended General-Purpose Gemma Generation

Gemma 3 is Google DeepMind's third-generation open-weight model, refining the instruction following, multilingual depth, and structured output that made Gemma 2 popular. Available at 1B, 4B, 12B, and 27B parameter counts, it covers the full range from minimal pilot hardware to GPU-assisted professional deployments.

For most organizations evaluating a general-purpose private AI assistant, Gemma 3 at 9B–12B on a Standard AI instance (4–8 GB) is the current recommended starting point in the Gemma family.

Who this model family is for

Four Perspectives on Deploying Gemma 3

👤 Personal use

A current-generation assistant that scales to your machine

Gemma 3 gives you Google's newest open Gemma quality across a wide size ladder, so you can run a 1B model on a tiny instance or a 12B on a modest server and get genuinely capable drafting, summarising, and Q&A. Its strong multilingual coverage makes it a good pick if you work across languages day to day — all running privately, with nothing sent to a cloud service.

🏢 Business & team use

The recommended general-purpose Gemma for most teams

For organisations standardising on a private assistant, Gemma 3 at 12B on a Standard AI instance is the current Gemma sweet spot — stronger reasoning and instruction following than Gemma 2 at a similar footprint. The 30+ language coverage suits international teams handling correspondence and documentation across markets without routing any of it externally.

⚙️ Developer & technical use

Improved structured output and optional vision

Gemma 3's better instruction following and structured output make it a more reliable engine for templated workflows and JSON-style responses than the previous generation. The 4B and larger variants are multimodal, so the same family can also handle image and document-understanding tasks — useful when you want one deployment to cover both text and basic vision work. Licensing information

License Summary

All Gemma 3 sizes are released under the Gemma Terms of Use, Google's custom open-weight license. It permits commercial use and redistribution but is not OSI-approved open source: it carries a Prohibited Use Policy and attribution requirements that follow the model into anything you build on it. The terms are consistent across 1B, 4B, 12B, and 27B — including the multimodal variants — so review the policy once before commercial deployment.
