GPT-2 vs Modern Local LLMs

How far has open-source AI come since 2019? We put the model that started the conversation up against today's best locally-runnable alternatives.

Last updated: July 2025  |  Tested models: GPT-2 XL, Llama 3.2 3B, Mistral 7B, Phi-3 Mini, Gemma 2 2B

The Short Answer

GPT-2 was genuinely impressive in 2019. In 2025, it is completely outclassed by every modern local model on every practical task — and those modern models often run on less RAM and respond faster.

But GPT-2 still matters. It is the most studied language model in history, it runs on essentially any hardware, and understanding it makes everything that came after easier to grasp.

Six Years of Progress: A Timeline

2019 — GPT-2 (OpenAI)

1.5B parameters. OpenAI calls it "too dangerous to release" then releases it anyway. The world does not end. Text generation suddenly feels real.

2021 — GPT-J, GPT-NeoX (EleutherAI)

First serious open-source competitors. 6B parameters. Community proves it can match OpenAI's earlier work without the corporate wrapper.

2023 — LLaMA 1, Alpaca, Vicuna

Meta releases LLaMA weights. The open-source explosion begins. Instruction tuning makes models actually useful for conversation for the first time.

2023 — Mistral 7B

A 7B model beats GPT-3.5 on several benchmarks. The parameter count stops being the only thing that matters. Efficiency becomes the new competition.

2024 — Phi-3, Gemma, Llama 3

Small models get dramatically smarter. Phi-3 Mini (3.8B) beats models ten times its size on reasoning tasks. Ollama makes one-command local deployment standard.

2025 — Llama 3.3, Qwen 3, DeepSeek R1

Reasoning models arrive locally. 70B models run on consumer hardware. The gap between local and cloud AI narrows to near-zero for most practical tasks.

Where GPT-2 Still Wins

It wins on history, not performance. GPT-2 is the most documented, most studied, most written-about language model ever released. If you want to understand how LLMs work at a fundamental level, GPT-2 is the right starting point — not because it is good, but because everything about it has been explained to death.
  • Academic reproducibility — Every paper from 2019–2022 you might want to replicate probably used GPT-2
  • Minimal dependencies — Runs with just Transformers and PyTorch, nothing exotic
  • Teaching tool — Fine-tuning GPT-2 is the "Hello World" of LLM customization
  • Extreme hardware constraints — The 117M parameter version runs on a Raspberry Pi 4

Real Output Examples

Prompt: "Explain how transformers work in simple terms"

GPT-2 XL response (2019)

"Explain how transformers work in simple terms. The key to understanding how a transformer works is to understand the basic principle. A transformer is a device that changes the voltage of an electrical current..."

→ Went literal. Talked about electrical transformers. Classic GPT-2 drift.

Llama 3.2 3B response (2025)

"A transformer is a type of neural network that processes text by paying 'attention' to relationships between words — no matter how far apart they are in a sentence. Instead of reading left to right like older models, transformers look at all words simultaneously..."

→ Correct answer, clear explanation, followed the instruction.
