What this model family is

Meta's Fourth-Generation Open AI — Natively Multimodal, MoE-Efficient, Long-Context

Llama 4 represents a significant architectural step from earlier Llama generations. Where Llama 3 was a dense transformer, Llama 4 adopts a Mixture-of-Experts design that activates only a fraction of its parameters per token — delivering better output quality at lower active compute cost. The Scout model brings a 10M+ token context window, meaning entire codebases, document libraries, or long research corpora fit in a single context without chunking.

Equally important: Llama 4 is natively multimodal. Both Scout and Maverick process images alongside text without requiring a separate vision model. Organizations that need AI to handle documents with embedded images, visual Q&A, or screenshot analysis can now do it from a single private Llama 4 deployment.

What Makes Llama 4 the Right Choice for Private Deployment

Native vision without a second deployment

Llama 4's built-in vision capability means one deployment handles both text and images. Documents with embedded charts, screenshots, scanned forms, and visual diagrams are processed privately in a single inference call.

Frontier-competitive quality, private by default

Organizations that previously needed GPT-4o or Claude for demanding reasoning tasks now have a comparable open-weight option that runs on infrastructure they control. No prompts, documents, or images leave your server.

10M token context window (Scout)

Scout's 10M token context is orders of magnitude beyond previous Llama generations. Entire codebases, research libraries, or year-long document collections can be analyzed in a single context — eliminating the chunking complexity that causes AI pipelines to lose cross-document reference.

MoE efficiency — more capability per GPU hour

The Mixture-of-Experts design means Llama 4 delivers significantly more capability than a dense model of equivalent active parameter count. On-premise GPU investments go further with Llama 4 than with equivalent-quality dense alternatives.

Who this model family is for

Who This Model Family is For

👤 Personal use

Llama 4 is a strong pick if you want a capable home assistant that handles long documents, multi-step questions, and image understanding without sending anything to a cloud provider. The smaller Scout variant runs comfortably on a single high-end consumer GPU or a well-equipped workstation, making it realistic for hobbyists who already own decent hardware. Be aware that Llama 4 is a mixture-of-experts architecture, so RAM and VRAM requirements are higher than the parameter count alone suggests — you're loading all experts even if only some activate per token.

🏢 Business & team use

For small and mid-sized teams, Llama 4 Scout offers a practical balance: native multimodal input, a very long context window (useful for contracts, transcripts, and internal knowledge bases), and competitive instruction-following without the per-token costs of hosted APIs. Maverick is a better fit for organizations that need stronger reasoning across customer support automation, internal copilots, or document-heavy workflows. Keep in mind the Llama 4 Community License has commercial restrictions for very large companies (see below), which may affect enterprise buyers.

⚙️ Developer & technical use

Llama 4 is well-suited for building RAG systems, agentic workflows, and code-assist tools where long-context recall actually matters — Scout's 10M token context is genuinely useful for whole-repo analysis, though effective recall at the extremes is still an open question. Native image input simplifies multimodal pipelines that previously required separate vision models. Tool-calling and structured output work reliably, but expect to spend time on prompt engineering and evals; Llama 4 is less polished out-of-the-box than closed frontier models.

🔬 Data science & research

Researchers get an openly available MoE model with transparent weights, making it a rare option for studying expert routing, long-context behavior, and multimodal alignment at scale. Llama 4 is a reasonable baseline for fine-tuning experiments, distillation work, and comparative benchmarking against Llama 3, Mistral, and Qwen families. Limitations: training data details remain partial, and the largest Behemoth variant has not been released as open weights, which restricts certain reproducibility studies.

Licensing information

Licensing Information

Llama 4 is released under the Llama 4 Community License Agreement, an open-weights license with specific restrictions. Key points:

  • Free for most commercial and personal use, including fine-tuning and derivative models.
  • Acceptable Use Policy applies (no use for weapons, CSAM, mass surveillance, etc.).
  • 700M monthly active user threshold: companies whose products exceeded 700M MAU as of the Llama 4 release date must request a separate license from Meta.
  • Attribution required: derivative models must include "Llama" in their name and display "Built with Llama."
  • EU restriction: at release, Meta's license restricted multimodal model use for entities domiciled in the EU. Confirm current status before deploying in that region.
