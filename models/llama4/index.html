What this model family is

Meta's Fourth-Generation Open AI — Natively Multimodal, MoE-Efficient, Long-Context

Llama 4 represents a significant architectural step from earlier Llama generations. Where Llama 3 was a dense transformer, Llama 4 adopts a Mixture-of-Experts design that activates only a fraction of its parameters per token — delivering better output quality at lower active compute cost. The Scout model brings a 10M+ token context window, meaning entire codebases, document libraries, or long research corpora fit in a single context without chunking.

Equally important: Llama 4 is natively multimodal. Both Scout and Maverick process images alongside text without requiring a separate vision model. Organizations that need AI to handle documents with embedded images, visual Q&A, or screenshot analysis can now do it from a single private Llama 4 deployment.

Why teams choose it

What Makes Llama 4 the Right Choice for Private Deployment

Native vision without a second deployment

Llama 4's built-in vision capability means one deployment handles both text and images. Documents with embedded charts, screenshots, scanned forms, and visual diagrams are processed privately in a single inference call.

Frontier-competitive quality, private by default

Organizations that previously needed GPT-4o or Claude for demanding reasoning tasks now have a comparable open-weight option that runs on infrastructure they control. No prompts, documents, or images leave your server.

10M token context window (Scout)

Scout's 10M token context is orders of magnitude beyond previous Llama generations. Entire codebases, research libraries, or year-long document collections can be analyzed in a single context — eliminating the chunking complexity that causes AI pipelines to lose cross-document reference.

MoE efficiency — more capability per GPU hour

The Mixture-of-Experts design means Llama 4 delivers significantly more capability than a dense model of equivalent active parameter count. On-premise GPU investments go further with Llama 4 than with equivalent-quality dense alternatives.

Who this model family is for

Four Perspectives on Deploying Llama 4

👤 Personal use

Frontier-class quality, but for serious home hardware

Llama 4 is a GPU-class deployment, not a laptop model — even Scout expects a capable GPU. For enthusiasts with that hardware, it offers native vision and very long context in one private model, so a single deployment can read documents, images, and long files at home. If your machine is modest, a Llama 3.1 8B is the realistic personal choice and Llama 4 is the aspirational upgrade.

🏢 Business & team use

Frontier-competitive capability that stays in-house

For teams that previously reached for GPT-4o or Claude on demanding work, Llama 4 brings comparable quality onto infrastructure you control — with native vision so documents containing charts and scans are handled in one pass. Review the Llama 4 Community License before rollout: it permits commercial use but carries a large-platform (700M MAU) threshold and EU-specific multimodal restrictions worth checking for your context.

⚙️ Developer & technical use

Long-context, multimodal backend with MoE efficiency

Scout's very long context lets developers feed entire codebases or document libraries without chunking, and the Mixture-of-Experts design delivers more capability per GPU hour than a dense model of equivalent active size. Native vision means one endpoint handles text and images for tools that previously needed two. Llama also has the largest tooling and fine-tuning ecosystem of any open model.

🔬 Data science & research

Long-corpus synthesis and multimodal experimentation

The very long context window makes Llama 4 well suited to analysing whole document collections, research corpora, or multi-year archives in a single private session, while native vision supports multimodal experiments without a separate encoder. Maverick's larger expert count gives researchers a frontier-competitive open baseline for reasoning studies on infrastructure they fully control.

Licensing information

License Summary

Llama 4 is released under the Llama 4 Community License Agreement — a custom Meta license, not an OSI open-source license. It permits commercial use and redistribution with attribution ("Built with Llama"), but adds two conditions worth checking: organisations above roughly 700 million monthly active users must request a separate license from Meta, and the multimodal capability carries usage restrictions for entities domiciled in the EU. The same terms apply to both Scout and Maverick.
