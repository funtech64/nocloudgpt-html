Vicuna

The Classic Instruction-Tuned Llama — A Foundational Open AI Still Worth Deploying

Vicuna is an instruction-tuned Llama fine-tune from LMSYS (Language Model Systems) at UC Berkeley, trained on ShareGPT conversation data. Released in 2023, it was one of the first demonstrations that a fine-tuned open-weight model could approach GPT-3.5-class conversation quality. While largely superseded by newer models, Vicuna is occasionally still the right choice for specific compatibility or legacy deployment scenarios.

Vicuna is based on Llama 1 and Llama 2. For most new deployments, Nous Hermes, Llama 3.1, or Qwen will deliver better results. VERIFY: Confirm whether Vicuna is the right choice for your specific use case.

Who developed it

LMSYS / UC Berkeley

[LMSYS / UC Berkeley — developer badge]

Vicuna was developed by researchers at UC Berkeley's LMSYS group (Language Model Systems). Released in March 2023, it was a landmark demonstration of fine-tuning Llama 1 on GPT-generated conversation data to produce instruction-following capability without base model pretraining. The LMSYS group has since produced other notable work including Chatbot Arena.

Who this model family is for

Four Perspectives on Deploying Vicuna

Vicuna is a 2023 classic that newer models have largely overtaken. These perspectives are deliberately honest about when it still makes sense — and when it does not.

👤 Personal use

Mainly of historical interest for tinkerers

For most personal use, a current model like Llama 3.1 8B or Phi-4 will give you noticeably better answers on the same hardware. Vicuna is worth running if you are curious about open-source AI history or want to reproduce a 2023-era tutorial or experiment — its behaviour is among the most documented of any open model. For a daily assistant, start with something current.

🏢 Business & team use

Only for maintaining an existing Vicuna-based system

The honest business case is narrow: if you already have a production system or evaluation pipeline tuned around Vicuna's specific behaviour, a private Vicuna deployment may be cheaper than migrating right now. For any new business deployment, a current-generation model will deliver materially better quality and a cleaner upgrade path — NoCloudGPT will help you pick one.

⚙️ Developer & technical use

Compatibility shims and reproducible legacy stacks

Developers maintaining tooling, demos, or integrations built against Vicuna's prompt format and output style may find it simpler to run the original than to re-validate everything against a new model. Because it is based on Llama 1/2, its commercial use is governed by the underlying Llama license — confirm which base version your variant uses before shipping.

🔬 Data science & research

A well-documented historical baseline

Vicuna's real ongoing value is in research: it was a landmark demonstration that fine-tuning on conversation data could approach GPT-3.5-class quality, and it remains a clean, heavily-studied baseline for evaluating how far open models have progressed. For reproducibility studies and model-generation comparisons, Vicuna is a reasonable fixed reference point.

When to Choose a Different Model

For new deployments, use a current-generation model

Vicuna is based on Llama 1/2 and is significantly outperformed by Llama 3.1 8B, Nous Hermes 3, Qwen 4B, and Phi-4 on virtually all tasks. Choose Vicuna only for compatibility reasons. See Llama →

Licensing information

License Summary

Vicuna is a fine-tune of Meta's Llama, so its license follows the base model. The current Vicuna v1.5 (7B and 13B) is built on Llama 2 and is therefore governed by the Llama 2 Community License — commercial use permitted with attribution and the standard large-platform condition. The older v1.3 is built on Llama 1 and is restricted to non-commercial research use. Confirm which base your variant uses before deploying.
