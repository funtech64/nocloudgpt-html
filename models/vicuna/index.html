Vicuna

The Classic Instruction-Tuned Llama — A Foundational Open AI Still Worth Deploying

Vicuna is an instruction-tuned Llama fine-tune from LMSYS (Language Model Systems) at UC Berkeley, trained on ShareGPT conversation data. Released in 2023, it was one of the first demonstrations that a fine-tuned open-weight model could approach GPT-3.5-class conversation quality. While largely superseded by newer models, Vicuna is occasionally still the right choice for specific compatibility or legacy deployment scenarios.

Vicuna is based on Llama 1 and Llama 2. For most new deployments, Nous Hermes, Llama 3.1, or Qwen will deliver better results. NoCloudGPT will confirm current model availability, license terms, tags, and hardware fit before deployment begins.

Who developed it

LMSYS / UC Berkeley

Vicuna was developed by researchers at UC Berkeley's LMSYS group (Language Model Systems). Released in March 2023, it was a landmark demonstration of fine-tuning Llama 1 on GPT-generated conversation data to produce instruction-following capability without base model pretraining. The LMSYS group has since produced other notable work including Chatbot Arena.

Who this model family is for

Who This Model Family is For

👤 Personal use

Vicuna is a decent choice if you want a conversational local AI that feels relatively natural and helpful. Because it was fine-tuned on real human conversations from ShareGPT, it tends to produce more fluid, back-and-forth responses than many other open models. It works reasonably well for everyday writing, summarization, and general Q&A. However, for complex reasoning or serious coding work, newer models (such as Llama 3.3, Qwen 2.5, or DeepSeek V3) will give you noticeably better results.

🏢 Business / team use

Vicuna can be used for internal tools where the main need is clear, conversational output — such as drafting emails, summarizing meeting notes, or generating first-draft content. Its conversational style makes it feel more approachable than many technical models. That said, it is no longer among the strongest options for structured tasks, multi-step reasoning, or high-stakes business use. Teams that need reliability and accuracy should evaluate more recent models before committing to Vicuna.

⚙️ Developer & technical use

Developers can use Vicuna for quick prototyping of conversational interfaces or internal chat tools. It responds reasonably well to instructions and handles multi-turn conversations adequately. However, it lacks the strong function calling, structured output, and coding performance found in newer models. If your project involves building production-grade tools, agentic workflows, or code-related features, you will likely get better results from more current alternatives.

🔬 Data science & research

Vicuna holds historical value as one of the first widely popular fine-tunes that showed strong conversational ability from crowd-sourced data. Researchers studying instruction tuning, conversational behavior, or the evolution of open models may still find it useful as a reference point. For current research or production data work, however, newer models generally offer better performance and capabilities.

Licensing information

Licensing Information

Vicuna is built on top of Meta's LLaMA base models and is released under a research / non-commercial license. This means Vicuna is generally not approved for commercial use without explicit permission from Meta.

If your intended use is personal experimentation, academic research, or internal testing, you are usually within scope. If you plan to use Vicuna in a product, charge customers, or deploy it as part of a revenue-generating service, you should not use this model family without reviewing Meta's current license terms directly.
