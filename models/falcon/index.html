Falcon Family

Falcon — Sovereign Open AI With the License to Use It However You Need

Built by the UAE's Technology Innovation Institute and released under one of the most permissive licenses in open AI, Falcon and Falcon 3 give organizations genuine ownership over their AI deployment — no vendor dependency, no usage restrictions, no data leaving your environment.

When the Technology Innovation Institute in Abu Dhabi released Falcon in 2023, it briefly held the top position on the Hugging Face Open LLM Leaderboard and made every AI vendor paying attention to the open-weight space reassess their assumptions. Falcon was the first serious signal that state-backed research institutions outside the United States could produce models that competed with the frontier.

What makes Falcon strategically interesting for private deployment is not its benchmark position — which has evolved since its initial release — but two structural advantages that most open models do not share. First, Falcon carries one of the most permissive commercial licenses available in open AI: TII's Falcon License allows use, modification, and deployment without the restrictions that Llama's and others' community licenses impose. Second, Falcon was trained with genuine multilingual depth, not a post-hoc add-on, making it a materially stronger choice for organizations operating across Arabic, French, Spanish, German, and a range of other languages.

Falcon 3, released in late 2024, takes the family significantly further. It competes directly with Llama 3 and Gemma 2 at equivalent sizes while maintaining the licensing clarity that makes the Falcon family a safe long-term infrastructure choice for commercial deployments.

What Makes Falcon Structurally Different From Most Open Models

Most model families involve a trade-off: stronger licensing restrictions in exchange for stronger model capability. Meta's Llama models carry restrictions on how they can be used commercially at scale. Mistral's terms have evolved. Many open models require attribution or restrict derivative use in ways that complicate commercial integration.

Falcon's TII license does not impose these restrictions in the same way. For companies that need to integrate a model into a commercial product, build internal tooling they intend to white-label, or operate at scale without negotiating with a model provider, this distinction has real legal and procurement significance. Your legal team will have a cleaner path with Falcon than with most alternatives.

The second structural difference is training data composition. Falcon was built on RefinedWeb, a large, carefully curated multilingual dataset developed specifically for the project. This is not a dataset assembled from existing academic sources — it is purpose-built at scale, which shows up in the model's multilingual capabilities and its comparative performance on tasks requiring broad world knowledge rather than narrow benchmark optimization.

Where Falcon Consistently Delivers

Genuine multilingual capability — not just English plus tokens

Falcon's training data was built with multilingual depth by design. Arabic, French, Spanish, and German perform at a level that reflects actual training investment, not the sparse representation common in English-first models. Organizations with Middle Eastern, North African, or European operations will notice the difference in output quality immediately.

Broad knowledge and general reasoning

The RefinedWeb training approach produces a model with genuinely broad world knowledge that holds up across general business tasks: market research, competitive summarization, policy drafting, correspondence writing, and analytical tasks that require drawing on broad context rather than narrow domain expertise.

Falcon 3 — improved instruction following and reasoning

Falcon 3 delivers substantially improved instruction adherence, reasoning quality, and structured output formatting compared to the original Falcon models. Teams that evaluated the original Falcon family and found it below their requirements should re-evaluate with Falcon 3, which competes directly in the current generation of open models.

Permissive commercial licensing

For legal, procurement, and compliance teams reviewing AI deployment, Falcon's license is among the cleanest available in the open-weight space. This matters both for current deployment approval and for long-term risk management in commercial integrations.

Available across a practical size range

Falcon is available at 1B, 3B, 7B, 11B, and 180B parameter sizes, with Falcon 3 covering 1B through 10B efficiently. This gives organizations a clear path from edge deployments requiring minimal hardware to full-capability server deployments, all within a consistent model family.

Math and STEM improvement in Falcon 3

Falcon 3 shows significant improvement on mathematical and scientific reasoning benchmarks compared to Falcon 2. For organizations in engineering, scientific research, education, or technical fields, Falcon 3 is a materially more capable choice than the original Falcon models for quantitative work.

Who Should Be Running Falcon

Organizations operating in the Middle East and North Africa

Arabic language AI assistance has historically required either cloud APIs or large, expensive models. Falcon's training investment in Arabic means that organizations in the MENA region can run a private Arabic-capable AI assistant on modest infrastructure without routing sensitive communications through an external service. For government bodies, financial institutions, and private companies in the region, this is a significant capability shift.

The TII's involvement means there is also a degree of regional alignment in the model's training that matters for cultural and contextual accuracy in ways that are genuinely difficult to measure on benchmarks but visible in production use.

Companies building commercial AI products

For startups and software companies integrating a language model into a commercial product — whether a vertical SaaS tool, an internal enterprise application, or a consumer product — Falcon's licensing creates the cleanest path to production without ongoing negotiation with a model provider or the risk of license term changes affecting your business.

The practical reality for commercial AI products is that the model is infrastructure. Choosing a model with clear, stable licensing from a state-backed research institution reduces one class of business risk that model providers under commercial pressure can introduce without warning.

European businesses with multilingual requirements

French and Spanish performance in Falcon is notably stronger than in many equivalently sized English-primary models. For European organizations that need to produce, summarize, or respond to content across multiple EU languages, a single Falcon deployment can replace multiple language-specific tools or the need to route multilingual content through cloud translation and AI APIs.

Combined with the GDPR compliance benefit of keeping data on your own EU-region infrastructure, Falcon is a particularly practical choice for European companies that need both privacy and language coverage simultaneously.

Government and public sector organizations

State and local government bodies, public universities, and national research institutions face procurement requirements that private companies do not. Vendor lock-in, data sovereignty regulations, accessibility requirements, and procurement approval timelines all create barriers to cloud AI adoption. Falcon, running on infrastructure the agency controls, resolves most of these barriers at once.

The TII origin is also a consideration in some procurement contexts where country-of-origin requirements apply differently to different model families. Your procurement team should evaluate this in the context of your specific regulatory environment.

Research institutions and universities

Academic teams that need a capable AI assistant for research synthesis, literature review, and writing support benefit from Falcon's broad knowledge base and permissive licensing that allows modification and derivative research. Running Falcon on institutional infrastructure also keeps student and faculty data within the institution's own environment — which increasingly matters for institutional data governance policies.

Edge and embedded deployment teams

Falcon 3's 1B and 3B models are specifically designed for efficiency at small scale. Teams building AI capability into devices, field equipment, or disconnected environments — where a cloud API is unavailable and a full Llama 3 deployment is impractical — will find Falcon 3 small models deliver meaningful capability in very constrained footprints. The Falcon 3 1B model can run inference on hardware that costs under $100.

Falcon vs. the Alternatives — Where It Wins

Falcon does not claim to be the top-performing open model on every benchmark. That position changes with each new model release and is often benchmark-specific in ways that do not map cleanly to real work performance. What Falcon offers that most alternatives do not is a combination of licensing clarity, genuine multilingual depth, and a stable research institution backing that does not face commercial pressure to restrict open access.

Teams choosing between Falcon and Llama for a commercial integration should have their legal team compare the license terms carefully. Teams choosing between Falcon and Mistral for a multilingual European deployment should test both on their actual language pair and task type. Teams in the MENA region evaluating Arabic-capable models will find Falcon's Arabic performance is a meaningful differentiator relative to most alternatives at equivalent compute cost.

The choice to deploy Falcon is rarely purely about raw benchmark performance. It is about choosing a model that fits the legal, operational, and linguistic requirements of a real deployment — and Falcon occupies a distinctive position on all three dimensions.

Licensing Information

Falcon's licensing is a genuine strength, but it is not uniform across the family. The original Falcon 7B and 40B are Apache 2.0. Falcon 2 and Falcon 3 are released under the TII Falcon License 2.0 — a permissive, Apache-2.0-based license with an acceptable use policy, broadly commercial-friendly. The exception is the giant Falcon 180B, which carries a more restrictive bespoke license with hosting restrictions. Verify the specific model before commercial deployment.
