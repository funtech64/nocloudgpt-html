Vicuna: The Model That Proved
Fine-Tuning Works

In March 2023, the LMSYS team fine-tuned Llama 13B on 70,000 real ChatGPT conversations scraped from ShareGPT — and produced a model that human judges rated at 92% of ChatGPT quality. For $300 in compute. Over a weekend. Vicuna didn't just produce a good model; it proved that the open-source community could close the gap fast. Almost every subsequent model in this guide is a direct or indirect descendant of that insight.

Why Vicuna Mattered: A Brief History

To understand Vicuna, you need to understand what people believed in early 2023. The prevailing assumption was that truly capable chat models required vast proprietary datasets, enormous compute budgets, and specialised RLHF pipelines that only OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google could afford. Then, in a single month, three teams shattered that assumption in sequence.

🦙
February 2023
Meta releases Llama 1
Llama 1 (7B, 13B, 33B, 65B) is released for research. Within days it leaks publicly. The open-source community suddenly has access to a high-quality base model. The only missing piece: instruction following. Base Llama responds to prompts but doesn't behave like a chat assistant.
🌿
March 13, 2023
Stanford releases Alpaca
Stanford fine-tunes Llama 7B on 52,000 GPT-generated instruction-following examples for ~$600. Alpaca proves that instruction tuning is achievable at low cost. But quality is limited — Alpaca's training data consists of short, synthetic single-turn instructions, not real conversations. It shows the door; it doesn't walk through it.
🏆
March 30, 2023
LMSYS releases Vicuna-13B
LMSYS fine-tunes Llama 13B on 70,000 real ChatGPT conversations from ShareGPT — actual multi-turn dialogues from real users. The key insight: real conversations are richer and more diverse than synthetic instructions. GPT-4-judged evaluation rates Vicuna-13B at 92% of ChatGPT quality. The community's mental model of what's possible shifts overnight.
🏟️
April 2023
LMSYS launches Chatbot Arena
To evaluate Vicuna and subsequent models rigorously, LMSYS builds Chatbot Arena — a blind A/B testing platform where humans judge model pairs without knowing which is which. The resulting Elo rankings become the community's most trusted capability benchmark. MT-Bench (the scale used throughout this guide) is also created by LMSYS to complement Arena.
📈
July 2023
Vicuna-1.5 on Llama 2
When Meta releases Llama 2, LMSYS quickly produces Vicuna-1.5 — fine-tuned on Llama 2 7B and 13B. Context window expands from 2K to 4K. Performance improves. But by this point, a dozen other fine-tuning efforts have emerged using the same ShareGPT approach, many with improvements Vicuna's team hasn't incorporated.
🌊
Late 2023 – 2024
Vicuna superseded — but its legacy continues
Mistral 7B (October 2023), OpenChat 3.5 (November 2023), and Llama 3 (April 2024) all significantly outperform Vicuna-1.5. Vicuna is no longer the best choice for any practical task, but the approach it pioneered — fine-tuning on real conversations — is now standard practice. Its ShareGPT data is embedded in the training sets of dozens of descendants.
📚
Vicuna's lasting contribution: evaluation infrastructure

The most enduring part of LMSYS's work is not Vicuna itself but the infrastructure built around it. MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena are both LMSYS creations, and both remain the primary evaluation frameworks used throughout this guide and the broader open-source AI community. Every benchmark score on every page of this guide traces directly back to the methodology LMSYS developed to validate Vicuna's claims.

How Vicuna Was Built

Vicuna's training recipe was simple enough to fit in a blog post and reproduce on a weekend — which is exactly why it was so influential. The key innovations over Alpaca were in data quality, not training technique.

Training data: ShareGPT conversations

ShareGPT was a website where ChatGPT users publicly shared their conversations. The LMSYS team scraped approximately 70,000 of these conversations. Unlike Alpaca's single-turn synthetic instructions, ShareGPT conversations are:

  • Multi-turn — real back-and-forth dialogue spanning multiple exchanges
  • User-initiated — prompts reflect what real users actually want, not what researchers think they want
  • Diverse — covering coding, writing, Q&A, roleplay, summarisation, and everything in between
  • Long-form — many conversations include detailed, structured responses that teach format as well as content

Training modifications

LMSYS made several technical improvements over the basic instruction-tuning approach used in Alpaca:

  • Gradient checkpointing — reduces memory usage during training, allowing longer sequences to fit in GPU memory
  • Loss computed only on model responses — the training loss is masked on user-turn tokens; the model only learns to predict the assistant's side of the conversation. This was a key fix over naive approaches that trained on all tokens equally
  • Sequence length expansion — ShareGPT conversations are often long; Vicuna's training pipeline handles sequences up to 2,048 tokens (later extended in v1.5)
  • SponsorBlock-style data cleaning — conversations with hallucinated context, heavily truncated content, or other quality issues were filtered

The gap is not a criticism of Vicuna

Every model above it in this table owes its existence, at least in part, to Vicuna's demonstration that ShareGPT-style fine-tuning works. Vicuna didn't fail to keep up — it pioneered the race it's now falling behind in.

The Licence Problem

⚠️ Why Vicuna Cannot Be Used Commercially

Vicuna carries two non-commercial restrictions that stack on each other:

1. Llama 1 base (v1.0, v1.3): Meta's Llama 1 licence prohibited commercial use entirely. Any Vicuna version based on Llama 1 inherits this restriction regardless of what LMSYS intended.

2. ShareGPT training data: OpenAI's Terms of Service prohibit using ChatGPT outputs to train competing models. The ShareGPT conversations used to train Vicuna are ChatGPT outputs. Vicuna-1.5 attempts to address the base model issue by switching to Llama 2, but the ShareGPT training data problem remains. LMSYS acknowledges this and released Vicuna under CC BY NC 4.0 explicitly because of it.

Practical guidance: For research, personal projects, and educational use — Vicuna is fine. For anything commercial, customer-facing, or deployed in a business context, use OpenChat 3.5 or Starling 7B instead. Both achieve superior MT-Bench scores under Apache 2.0 licences.

Honest Assessment

✓ Genuine Strengths

  • Historical significance — understand this model to understand the field
  • Well-documented training methodology; excellent for learning fine-tuning
  • LMSYS provenance — same team as MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena
  • Stable, widely tested; extensive community documentation
  • Conversational quality still adequate for personal research tasks
  • Multiple sizes available (7B, 13B, 33B) for different hardware
  • 4K context in v1.5 — double the original 2K limit

✗ Real Limitations

  • Non-commercial licence — blocks most production use cases
  • ShareGPT data: OpenAI ToS violation technically embedded in weights
  • Outperformed by every major model released after October 2023
  • 4K context is short by 2024 standards (vs 8K–128K alternatives)
  • No updates planned; LMSYS has moved on to Vicuna-based successors
  • Prone to verbosity and filler phrases inherited from ChatGPT data
  • Weaker on reasoning and code than purpose-built models
