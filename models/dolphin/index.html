Dolphin

Community Fine-Tuned AI for Open-Context Private Workflows

Dolphin is a series of fine-tuned models from Eric Hartford, built on top of Llama, Mistral, and other base models. Dolphin models reduce the default instruction restrictions of the base models, making them popular for creative writing, research, unrestricted Q&A, and internal workflows where the default guardrails of commercial models interfere with legitimate use cases.

Dolphin is produced by Eric Hartford, a member of the open-source AI community. The fine-tunes use synthetic instruction data to train models with reduced default refusals and more open context following. Dolphin models are popular in the homelab, creative writing, and research communities.

Who this model family is for

Four Perspectives on Deploying Dolphin

👤 Personal use

A homelab assistant that follows instructions without nagging

Dolphin is popular with homelab users who find commercial assistants over-eager to refuse. Running a 7B–8B variant on a single GPU gives you a private, steerable assistant for creative writing, research, and open-ended questions. Because the restrictions are removed, you are responsible for how you use it — running it on your own server keeps that firmly in your control.

🏢 Business & team use

Teams that want to own the policy layer, not inherit one

Dolphin suits internal tools where a base model's default refusals get in the way of legitimate work, and the team prefers to apply its own guardrails and access controls. It is deliberately not for public-facing products without an added safety layer. Licensing follows the underlying base model (Llama, Mistral, or Qwen), so confirm the specific variant's terms before commercial use.

⚙️ Developer & technical use

A compliant, steerable base for custom guardrail design

For developers building agents or internal tools, Dolphin's reduced default refusals make it predictable to steer with system prompts rather than fighting built-in behaviour. Pair it with your own classifier or a dedicated safety model at the edge. The various base models mean you can pick an Apache 2.0 (Mistral-based) variant when you need the cleanest license for embedding.

🔬 Data science & research

Open-ended exploration without false refusals

Researchers studying sensitive but legitimate topics — security, content moderation, social science, red-teaming — often hit false refusals from safety-tuned models. Dolphin's reduced guardrails make it useful for that exploratory work in a controlled environment, and its synthetic-instruction training approach is itself a documented reference for studying instruction tuning and alignment behaviour.

Honest guidance

When to Choose a Different Model

You need built-in content safety for public-facing AI

Dolphin is designed for private internal use with your own access controls. For public-facing applications, a model with built-in safety layers or an explicit safety classifier is more appropriate. See Llama Guard →

Licensing information

License Summary

Dolphin is a fine-tune, not a base model, so its license is inherited from whatever base model the specific variant was trained on. There is no single "Dolphin license" — a Mistral- or Mixtral-based Dolphin is Apache 2.0, a Llama-based Dolphin follows the relevant Llama Community License, and a Qwen-based Dolphin follows the Qwen license. Always check the exact variant's model card before commercial deployment.
