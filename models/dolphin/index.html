Dolphin

Community Fine-Tuned AI for Open-Context Private Workflows

Dolphin is a series of fine-tuned models from Eric Hartford, built on top of Llama, Mistral, and other base models. Dolphin models reduce the default instruction restrictions of the base models, making them popular for creative writing, research, unrestricted Q&A, and internal workflows where the default guardrails of commercial models interfere with legitimate use cases.

Who developed it

Eric Hartford (Community)

Dolphin is produced by Eric Hartford, a member of the open-source AI community. The fine-tunes use synthetic instruction data to train models with reduced default refusals and more open context following. Dolphin models are popular in the homelab, creative writing, and research communities.

Who this model family is for

Who This Model Family is For

👤 Personal use

Dolphin is a strong choice if you want a local AI that answers questions directly without adding unsolicited disclaimers or refusing legitimate requests. It was specifically tuned to be highly compliant and helpful, making it popular for creative writing, research on sensitive topics, role-playing, and general use where you want the model to follow instructions rather than lecture. Because it has fewer built-in guardrails, it rewards users who prompt thoughtfully and review outputs carefully.

🏢 Business / team use

Teams that need a cooperative assistant for internal drafting, policy work, or customer-facing content in complex or regulated industries will find Dolphin more willing to engage with difficult topics than heavily aligned models. It follows multi-step instructions reliably and rarely refuses reasonable requests. However, because it is less self-censoring, your own content policies and human review become the primary safeguards. It is not the best fit for high-stakes customer service or environments where strict output filtering is required out of the box.

⚙️ Developer & technical use

Developers building custom agents, tools, or applications often choose Dolphin because it responds predictably to system prompts and gets out of the way. This makes it easier to define your own behavior rules in code rather than fighting the model's built-in refusals. It performs well on coding tasks, tool use, and agentic workflows where consistent instruction following matters more than cautious hedging.

🔬 Data science & research

Researchers studying AI alignment, refusal behavior, instruction following, or red-teaming will find Dolphin a useful reference point because it sits at the high-compliance end of the spectrum by design. It is also practical for generating synthetic data or testing model behavior under fewer constraints. Some Dolphin variants are based on older base models, so capability varies significantly by version — always check the specific base model when using it for research.

Licensing information

Licensing Information

Dolphin models are fine-tunes created by Eric Hartford (ehartford). Licensing depends on the base model used for each variant:

  • Variants based on Meta's LLaMA 2 or LLaMA 3 are subject to Meta's LLaMA Community License (commercial use permitted only under 700 million monthly active users, with redistribution restrictions).
  • Variants based on Mistral are generally released under the permissive Apache 2.0 license.
  • Variants based on other architectures (Phi, Qwen, etc.) carry their respective upstream licenses.

You must verify the license of the specific Dolphin variant you intend to use. The fine-tune itself does not override the base model's license terms.
