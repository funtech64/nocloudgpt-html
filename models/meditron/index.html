What Is Meditron?

Meditron is a family of open-weight large language models developed by the EPFL LLM Team, fine-tuned specifically for medical and clinical knowledge. Built on the LLaMA 2 base and trained on a curated corpus that includes PubMed abstracts, medical guidelines, and clinical literature, Meditron was designed to bring capable medical reasoning to self-hostable models.

It comes in two sizes: Meditron 7B for resource-efficient local deployment, and Meditron 70B for deeper clinical reasoning on demanding benchmarks. Unlike general-purpose models that happen to know some medicine, Meditron was purpose-built for the domain — making it a focused tool for healthcare researchers, medical educators, and clinical software developers who need medical AI that stays local.

Why local matters for medical AI: Patient symptoms, diagnostic hypotheses, clinical notes, and research data all carry significant privacy implications. Routing these through a cloud API means trusting a third party with some of the most sensitive queries anyone makes. A locally deployed Meditron processes everything on your hardware — no API logging, no third-party access, no data leaving your network.

Model Variants

Meditron 7B

7B Parameters

The practical entry point for local medical AI. Runs on consumer GPUs with 6GB VRAM. Suitable for medical education, literature review, patient communication drafting, and healthcare IT prototyping. Fast enough for interactive use on modest hardware.

Meditron 70B

70B Parameters

The full-capability variant for serious clinical reasoning tasks. Requires substantial hardware — 48GB+ VRAM — but delivers significantly deeper medical knowledge and more reliable clinical reasoning on demanding benchmark tasks. For organizations with GPU infrastructure, this is the version that competes with the strongest available medical models.

What Meditron Is Good At

Medical Question Answering

Meditron was specifically trained on medical literature to answer clinical and biomedical questions accurately. It handles disease mechanisms, pharmacology, diagnostic criteria, and treatment guidelines with domain knowledge that general-purpose models lack. For medical education and reference tasks, the gap between Meditron and an unspecialized model is often significant.

Clinical Reasoning Support

The 70B variant demonstrates strong performance on clinical reasoning benchmarks — working through differential diagnoses, synthesizing presenting symptoms, and applying clinical guidelines. This is reference and reasoning support, not autonomous clinical decision-making, but for the right use cases the reasoning depth is genuinely useful.

Medical Literature Review

Researchers can query Meditron about study findings, pharmacological interactions, and treatment efficacy data from the medical literature it was trained on — keeping research queries and preliminary findings within a private environment rather than exposing them to commercial APIs.

Healthcare IT Development

Developers building clinical NLP features, medical knowledge bases, or healthcare software can use Meditron locally to prototype and test medical reasoning flows without exposing proprietary data or patient information to external services during development.

Meditron vs. MedGemma

Both are purpose-built medical models available for local deployment. The key differences:

Feature Meditron MedGemma
Developer EPFL LLM Team Google DeepMind
Base model LLaMA 2 Gemma
Sizes 7B, 70B 4B, 27B
Training focus PubMed, medical guidelines, clinical notes Medical imaging + text
Multimodal No Yes (image + text)
70B variant Yes No

If your use case involves medical images or radiology alongside text, MedGemma has a clear advantage. For pure clinical text reasoning — and especially if you need a 70B-scale medical model — Meditron remains the stronger choice. Both run via Ollama and can be compared side by side in Open WebUI.

Deploying Meditron via Ollama

# Meditron 7B — recommended starting point
ollama pull meditron

# Meditron 70B — serious clinical reasoning tasks
ollama pull meditron:70b

# Start Open WebUI
docker run -d -p 3000:8080 \
  -e OLLAMA_BASE_URL=http://localhost:11434 \
  ghcr.io/open-webui/open-webui:main

Once running, open http://localhost:3000, select meditron from the model dropdown, and begin your session. No accounts, no API keys, no data transmission.

Clinical Responsibility Notice

Meditron is a research and reference tool. It must not serve as the sole basis for clinical diagnosis, treatment planning, drug prescribing, or emergency medical guidance. Medical AI — including purpose-built models like Meditron — can produce confident but incorrect output. Always verify against authoritative clinical sources and licensed medical professionals. Local deployment eliminates the privacy concern; it does not remove the need for clinical judgment.
